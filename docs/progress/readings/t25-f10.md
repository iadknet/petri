# T25.F10 — Group Genes Cost as Genes: readings

## Options weighed at planning (2026-10-20)

| Question | Options | Chosen, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How a gene's members are priced | (a) split the four size costs by biological role: copy and mutation on total length (Avida, Tierra), maintenance on expressed structure; (b) one unit per group record, members free; (c) keep F02's uniform rule and pay for T27.F02's 28 silent edges per recruitment | (b), the user's choice (design note §8, 2026-10-20), first with a template-and-exception rule, then simplified the same day to one unit with no change tracking; the T26–T28 review's round 5 finding 3 challenged it and the user kept it, labelled an explicit economic discount. Precedent for charging the generator and not the phenotype: indirect encodings (HyperNEAT's CPPN genome is sized by the CPPN, not the substrate it wires; Stanley, D'Ambrosio, Gauci 2009, cited from memory). |
| How a fact member is marked | a stored role on `Membership`; a role read from the member's sink (`Drive` against a direction sink) | Read from the sink: the coordinate of every member is already structural (a ring channel, a pair field and direction), no field, no `GENOME_FORMAT` or protocol bump, and a stored role could disagree with the sink. |
| Why a fact member weighs its own weight | pattern 0 by role; a group term skipped for fact members | Pattern 0 by role through the one `compose` formula: F04 already defines a zero-pattern member as valid and inert, `learned.rs` credits `pattern × Δ`, so every reader stays one path. |
| Who builds the gene | recruitment adds the group then pushes facts as ungrouped edges and the container groups them; one container primitive builds record, direction members and fact members | One primitive: the container owns the group invariants (track criterion 1), and a two-site build would leave a window where the facts are not members. |
| Whether `AddDirectionPairGroup` also wires facts | yes (every pair gene carries facts); no (recruitment only) | No: the track Notes say ordinary slot-fact wiring outside a recruitment is unchanged, and the operator's draw and delta are pinned by F04/F08 tests. |
| `ComplexityEffect` of `DetachGraphEdge` (+1 unit under F10) | relabel `Increasing`; keep `Neutral` | Keep: the label names the effect on functional structure, which the size pressure gate restricts, and detach changes no edge or node count; `RemoveGraphEdge` on a member is likewise `Decreasing` at Δ 0. Stated as a Decision. |
| Where T27.F02's bloat counts come from | add them in T27.F02; add `facts` and `own_nonzero` to `pair_groups` now | Now, as `pair-groups-v2`: F10 creates the economic condition and the counts are two filters over the members F10 already iterates. |
| Commit order | size rule first, fact members second; fact members first | Fact members first: commit 1 changes genome text and a few draws but no size; commit 2 is the one cause every size reader moves under. |

## Rulings on the benchmark readings (spec owner, 2026-10-21)

- **Confluence size and supply rise (`mean_genome_size` 120.60 vs 110.64, attempted events per birth 0.6210 vs 0.5834).** Not a contradiction of F10's claim: the claim's falsifiers (founder 80, founder-only digests, a member counting a unit, a fact member weighing through its scale) all hold, and the Performance predeclaration stated the cross-run direction as a hypothesis, not an identity. The genes carried cannot account for the rise: Confluence holds 734 reachable pair genes and 0.758 ring genes per creature at tick 2,000 (229 and 0.296 at F09), about one unit per creature under the gene rule against a ten-unit rise; the rest is the other structure of a population that diverged under the changed draw stream, which the `genome_size` series entry marks as not like-for-like. The three-fold rise in genes carried where pair recruitment flourishes is the first short-horizon reading of the bloat risk the track note named; it is the baseline T27.F02's deferred reading compares with. Not a blocker; no user acceptance needed.
- **Goal wall clock (+76.33%).** A non-fatal flag whose mechanism is recorded (host load 19 to 23 on 8 cores with sibling worktrees running test binaries during the measurement, every phase up including `food_growth_ms`, which F10 does not touch). Not a blocker, no acceptance; not re-run (once per closure; its counters are deterministic and unaffected).
- **Gate wall clock (first run +17.56% on a loaded host, level ok; quiet-host re-run +31.72%, level flag, not fatal, counters byte-identical and below F09).** The first ruling ("inside the predeclared 25% band") was superseded by the re-run. A flag is not severe, so the workflow's acceptance rule does not fire and closure is not blocked by it; but the predeclaration says a flag is reported with its mechanism, and the mechanism is not isolated (the quieter run is slower in `cognition_ms` and `food_growth_ms` on every seed, so load average alone does not explain it, and F10 adds no per-tick work outside passive fact edges, which the 75-tick gate barely carries). Ruling: one paired attribution reading before the mutation gate, the workflow's parent-comparison method — build the merge base `ced01959` (F09 code) in a detached scratch worktree, run the gate workload back to back with the final code on the same host, two alternating pairs (F09, F10, F09, F10), and record each wall per creature-tick and the median F10/F09-now ratio here (about three minutes of build plus seconds of runs; the gate is not under the once-per-closure rule).
