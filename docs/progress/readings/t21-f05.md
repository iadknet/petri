| Decision | Chosen | Against | Why |

| Verdict statistic at a 5% ceiling on a noisy host | the median of the paired ratios with its 90% distribution-free interval (order statistics at the binomial ranks), pass when the upper bound is below the ceiling | (a) F01's median with ⌈0.75 n⌉ pairs under the ceiling at n = 5 or 8; (b) a 10% trimmed mean with a seeded bootstrap interval (the first draft, dropped after challenge round 1); (c) mean of log ratios with a t-interval; (d) min-of-k per side | F03, F04 and F06 measured reference spreads of 11–22% over three runs; the expected range of three normal draws is 1.69σ, so σ per run was 6.5–13% and σ per pair ratio (√2 σ) 9–18%. Under (a) at n = 8 the required ⌈0.75 n⌉ = 6 pairs under 1.05 is met with probability about 50% when the true ratio is 1.00 and σ is 12% (each pair lands under it with probability P(Z ≤ 0.05/0.12) ≈ 66%), so the routine rule cannot decide at 5% at that n; as n grows the median's interval does converge: its upper bound is an exact order statistic (the 22nd smallest of 32, the empirical 69th percentile), which under a normal heuristic sits about 0.49σ above the median, near the width a trimmed mean's bootstrap bound (about 1.645 × 1.05σ/√32 ≈ 0.31σ) would give, so (b) buys little the workflow's named statistic does not, and the order-statistic interval needs no random draw. (c) shifts by ln(r)/n on one load spike; (d) has no interval and reads the unloaded best case. Kalibera and Jones ("Rigorous benchmarking in reasonable time", ISMM 2013) and Criterion.rs are the references for interval-based verdicts on noisy hosts. |
| Pair count from spread | five reference runs; n = 16 at spread ≤ 5%, 24 at ≤ 10%, 32 at ≤ 20%; one repeat of the spread block above 20%, then inconclusive | F01's three runs and n = 5 or 8; a fixed n; a sequential stop when the interval is decisive; n = 40 in the top bucket (the first draft) | Heuristic sizing, not a guarantee of a decisive result: if run times were normal, five draws would have an expected range of 2.33σ, so the buckets would bound σ per run at about 2.1%, 4.3% and 8.6% and σ per ratio at 3%, 6% and 12%. The exact interval is the predeclared one: at n = 16, 24 and 32 the upper bound is the 12th, 17th and 22nd smallest ratio (k = 4, 7, 10), that is the empirical 75th, 71st and 69th percentile of the cell's ratios, so a cell passes when at least 12, 17 or 22 of its ratios are below 1.05. Under the normal heuristic that percentile sits about 0.67σ, 0.55σ and 0.49σ above the median: 2.0, 3.3 and 5.9 points in the three buckets, so a pass in the noisiest bucket needs a median at or below about 0.99, which F01–F02's 0.994–1.010 straddles: the top bucket can decide only when the host is kinder than its spread suggests, and inconclusive is the honest outcome otherwise. n = 40 with genuine pairs would put the six cells at about 3,400 s and leave no room for the presets inside the cap. A sequential stop was rejected as an extension rule; n is fixed before the first pair. |
