# T27.F03 — Giving Energy: readings

## Bite-size payoff calculation (predeclared, 2026-10-21)

Design review finding 4 asked for this before F03. Inputs: the T25.F09 goal
summary (`docs/progress/features/t25-f09-learned-weight-decay-goal.json`,
`predation` block per case), the effort floor `energy.costs.noop_cost` 0.4
(T16.F02; every action's effort is `max(base, 0.4)` times the complexity and
age multiplier `m`, 1.0 for a founder inside the grace window), and the
precedent (Taylor-Davies et al. 2024: one "feed" moves 20 units at an action
cost of 1.0, a 20:1 ratio).

Recorded victim-hit rates of blind steal attempts (every live steal requests
0 energy, so nothing was transferred):

| World (F09 goal case) | attempted | `Transferred` | hit rate `p` | attacker credit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Orchards (0) | 122,603 | 4,957 | 4.04% | 0.000 |
| Canyon (1) | 7,071 | 659 | 9.32% | 0.000 |
| Confluence (2) | 122,140 | 6,897 | 5.65% | 0.726 |

With a fixed bite `b` and the effort floor, a blind attempt's expected net is
at most `p·b − 0.4·m` (an upper bound: the transfer is `min(b, victim
energy)`), a targeted hit nets `b − 0.4·m`, and a NoOp costs `0.4·m` anyway,
so the attempt's own cost is what finding 4 compared against. In base units
(`m = 1`):

| `b` | blind net, Orchards / Confluence / Canyon | targeted hit net | `b` ÷ floor |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | −0.36 / −0.34 / −0.31 | 0.6 | 2.5 |
| 5 | −0.20 / −0.12 / +0.07 | 4.6 | 12.5 |
| **8** | **−0.08 / +0.05 / +0.35** | **7.6** | **20** |
| 10 | +0.00 / +0.17 / +0.53 | 9.6 | 25 |
| 20 | +0.41 / +0.73 / +1.46 | 19.6 | 50 |

Chosen: **8.0**. The upper bounds put blind stealing at today's hit rates
near break-even (−0.08 to +0.35 per attempt before the `min` with the
victim's energy pulls it down), so a bite of 8 does not make blind stealing
free money while a targeted hit nets 19 floors; whether stealing pays only
when targeted after partial transfers, multipliers and population change is
a measurement, not a result of this table (Codex round 1 F6). The
bite-to-floor ratio equals the one evolved-neural precedent, and a gift of 8
is 40% of the founder's starting energy (20), a quarter of the founder's
reproduce gate (32) and 20 ticks of NoOp effort for a starving recipient
(mean energy in the F09 cohorts was 24–33, review finding 5). A victim
holding less than 8 dies of one bite, so kills (0 in every recorded goal
run) can now occur. `steal_cost_rate` stays and scales the bite: at the
defaults `0.05 × 8 = 0.4` is exactly the floor, so the steal column's effort
in the table above is the floor; a higher rate or a larger bite raises it.

## Options weighed at planning (2026-10-21)

| Question | Options | Chosen, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bite size | 1 (finding 4's example), 5, 8, 10, 20 | 8 (table above). |
| Where the bite lives | `predation.bite`; `energy.costs`; a new `exchange` section | `exchange.bite`: one value both actions read; `predation` would misname the gift, and `costs` holds efforts, not amounts. `PredationConfig` keeps `kill_complexity_bonus_multiplier`. |
| Steal's effort | keep `steal_cost_rate × bite` (floored); retire the rate and charge the floor | Keep the rate (revised after Codex round 1 F6): the row fixes the mouthful, not steal's effort formula; the rate equals the floor only at the defaults (bite 20 at rate 0.05 is a base of 1.0), so retiring it would be an unrequested policy change, not a refactor. |
| A giver holding less than a bite | the give fails; it gives what it has | Gives what it has (`min(bite, energy)`, as steal takes `min(bite, victim energy)`): a parent can starve itself for the young, as `parental_transfer` deaths already show; a zero or non-positive remainder is the failure the row names. |
