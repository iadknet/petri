# T22 — Capability Assays and Evolvability Lab

**Status**: In Progress
**Last updated**: 2026-09-28
**Master**: [Program Roadmap](../roadmap.md)

## Goal

Answer "can creatures evolve this capability under Petri's own variation?"
in seconds to minutes instead of hours: small fixed arenas, real Petri
genomes, the production mutation engine, lab selection, and one capability
scored at a time. The lab measures reachability; the living world stays
natural and open-ended and never receives a lab task, reward or solution.
Its second job is exploration: before a track or feature exists, a session
tries many arenas, arms, starts and readings in minutes and writes a research
note that says what is worth a roadmap feature and why a capability is not
evolving.

## Track Success Criteria

- [ ] One command runs a capability assay on the development host in under a minute at quick-iteration sizes and reproduces byte-identical NDJSON for a seed.
- [ ] Food seeking and barrier navigation each have a calibrated arena: every scene presents the opportunity, the authored comparator beats the random-walk floor by a predeclared margin, and the scorer grades partial progress between them; the founder's position is recorded wherever it falls.
- [ ] Every lab run reports native-variation fidelity (production operator distribution, executed-biased targeting live, per-unit supply, identical-offspring fraction) on an unchanged-policy reference arm, classifies every policy-deviating arm as such, and carries its controls: founder-only, mutation-off, shuffled-score selection, authored comparator, random-walk floor.
- [ ] Each assay reports, per replicate, whether the capability was reached, the generation it was reached (censored when not), and what changed in the elite's brain and sensor use.
- [ ] A lab verdict may block or unblock roadmap rows, including mechanism rows that stay opt-in until qualified, and may retire opt-in, unqualified candidate code; it never sets a production default, founder, mutation policy or closure threshold. Production availability and any rate or targeting default still require goal-profile ecological evidence recorded in the changing feature's own spec; a lab result can suggest that change, never justify it alone. Lab readings appear in research notes and the evidence sections of feature specs.
- [ ] An exploration run (an existing assay with new parameters, arms, genome files or arena files) needs no roadmap feature: it yields a dated research note under `docs/strategy/` and, at most, an ordinary commit inside `crates/v3-lab` gated by `make check`.
- [ ] A why-not run names the first rung at which a capability stalls for a given start (exposure, supply, viability, benefit, retention) with the counts behind it, so the finding routes to the owning track.

## Executable Features

- [x] **T22.F01 — Lab Harness and Food-Seeking Assay** — Depends on: None
  - Goal: Measurement tooling. From one command, a small population of founders evolves on a fixed sparse-food arena under the production mutation engine and lab selection, and a seconds-scale report says whether food seeking improved, how fast, and against which controls.
- [ ] **T22.F02 — Barrier-Navigation Assay** — Depends on: T22.F01
  - Goal: Measurement tooling. The same harness scores whether lineages come to reach food behind barrier walls and rings without wasting moves, against an authored ring-inhibition comparator, the random-walk floor and the founder's barrier-blind start.
- [ ] **T22.F03 — Brain and Sensor Change Readings** — Depends on: T22.F01
  - Goal: Measurement tooling. Every lab run records what changed in each generation's elite: genome size, reachable and executed nodes, operator counts since the founder, sensor use by family, steering, and the score spread of its mutants.
- [ ] **T22.F04 — Why-Not Diagnostic Ladder** — Depends on: T22.F01, T22.F03
  - Goal: Measurement tooling. One command takes a capability and a start and reports the first rung at which evolution stalls, exposure, supply, viability, benefit or retention, with the counts behind it, so "branching is not evolving" becomes a routed finding instead of a guess.

- Why-not ladder (F04). "X is not evolving" decomposes into five rungs, each an existing reading: exposure (the arena presents the opportunity and the scorer responds to the authored comparator; a failure here is an instrument problem), supply (the native engine proposes changes touching the relevant nodes or families at all: requested, applied and skipped events with skip reasons, by operator), viability (proposals that touch it are silent, changed or dead on the battery and the mutant signature), benefit (a viable change moves the score; effect size and partial-progress sensitivity), and retention (a selected change persists under continued native mutation and lab selection). F04 reports the first failing rung for a capability and start, with the counts, and routes it: exposure to the assay, supply to T11/T13, viability to T11/T17, benefit to the scorer or, in the world, to the pressure tracks, retention to T13/T14. F04's spec defines, per rung, the observable predicate, its conditional denominator (relevant sites exposed, never operator totals alone), the evidence threshold, and the `inconclusive` disposition when exposure is insufficient, with fixtures that separate adjacent rungs. Retention is read over identified descendant lineages at declared mutation depths, never over an elite carried unchanged, and distinguishes deletion, lineage loss and censoring; the ancestry bookkeeping comes from F01's per-individual parent identity and applied-event counts. F04 adds no new reading beyond the ladder's bookkeeping.
- F01 contract. Inputs are data, so an exploration needs no Rust: every size is a CLI parameter; an arm is a named `SimulationConfig` overlay applied on top of the lab arena config (mutation off, a supply multiplier, an operator-weight variant) with an optional genome file as its start or its authored comparator, and the built-in controls are arms of this kind. Every run carries the unchanged-policy reference arm (the production `MutationConfig` with no overlay on supply, operator weights, targeting or operators); any arm whose overlay changes mutation policy is classified `policy-deviation` in every verdict and summary and is never reported as native reachability. Arena: 48²–64² from built-in seeded generators (F02 adds layout files), production defaults with only layout and initial state overridden (static food, growth and recovery zero; seeded placement per scene). Evaluation: each genome alone per scene from fresh state for a fixed lifetime, starting at world tick 62,680 (the failed-action ramp's default target, so every evaluation runs at the production penalty endpoint), reproduction suppressed by the `min_reproduce_energy` override (test precedent, no core change) with the rejected vote's failed-action penalty retained and recorded. Scoring: solo evaluation makes the simulation's per-tick counters the creature's, so the harness accumulates food intake by type, action results, energy and position between ticks and records the death tick; the action log is not the evidence path. Scores are graded per scene. Calibration gate before any campaign, checking the instrument rather than the founder: exposure on every scene, authored-comparator competence above the random-walk floor by a predeclared margin, and score sensitivity between them; the founder's position is recorded wherever it falls, the grid-selection rule, held-out validation scenes and the `uncalibrated` disposition (stop and report) are predeclared. Variation: `MutationEngine::apply_mutations_with_food_type_count` with the production `MutationConfig`, `mesh_reachable_nodes` of the parent, and `ParentExecuted::Record` frozen as the parent's dispatch record at the end of its last evaluated scene, resolved at that scene's age with the production window and snapshotted every tick so a death inside the scene still yields the last living record; union across scenes is a separately labelled diagnostic. Requested, applied and skipped events, exact genotype identity with the parent, and elite carry-overs are reported separately; neutral offspring are preserved; mutation, selection and observation use separate seeded RNG streams. A forced-event or ×k supply arm is a labelled diagnostic, never the reported number. Selection: truncation with elites only; the spec predeclares the selection scalar, the reach threshold, the held-out validation rule, the replicate (one seed lineage-population) as the sole independent unit, and the separation of horizon-censored failures from cap-stopped runs. Controls every run: founder-only, mutation-off, shuffled-score selection, authored comparator (labelled, never a start), random-walk floor. Outputs: one NDJSON row per replicate per generation, one compact keep-list summary with censored generation-to-threshold, a Wilson interval on the reached fraction and a provenance block (source revision and dirty state, resolved config digest, each overlay's content and application order, genome and arena content hashes with schema versions, seeds), per-individual parent identity and applied-event counts in the NDJSON so descendant lineages are identifiable, elite genomes at the end only, all under the telemetry rule above. An end-to-end solo-evaluation pilot replaces the research note's projected runtimes before quick-run sizes are fixed. A seconds-scale fixture at tiny sizes runs inside `make check`; real runs never do.
- F02 reuses F01's harness and adds arena layouts loadable from a JSON file (food, barrier and start cells, so a new arena needs no Rust) and built-in arenas with barrier walls and rings between creature and food, scores for blocked-move fraction and distance closed, and the existing authored area-food and ring-inhibition controllers from `neighborhood::opportunity::controllers` as the authored comparator, not a ceiling: each arena must be shown solvable and the comparator competent on it before the calibration gate passes. Lifetime learning is disabled by the clone-only expression mask unless an arm enables and separately attributes it; improvement across generations is inherited change, never described as learning.
- F03 adds per-elite readings on the same runs using existing `v3-core` observation seams: `functional_complexity`, `mesh_reachable_nodes`, `MutationSummary`, and three sensor readings kept distinct (structural reach from `creature_sensor_census`, executed use from the tracer-backed `neighborhood::mesh_execution` and `input_use` machinery, causal influence from ablation on the same scenes), plus the mutant signature (N mutants of the elite scored on the same scenes: score spread and silent/changed/dead fractions, after Tarapore and Mouret 2015). Any adapter is observation-only. The evolvability signature is a secondary reading; the headline stays score, generation to threshold and what changed.

- F02 finding (user decision, 2026-09-28): the composed area-food + ring-inhibition comparator (`controllers::controller` Vector plus the Ring edges) is not competent on `ring-v1`: it wins 9/10/5 of 16 calibration scenes at scales 1/2/3 against the random-walk floor (12 needed), because it has no wall-following and finds the gap only when the founder's own move votes drift it there; on `wall-v1` it is competent at scales 1–2 and not at 3 (6 of 16). T22.F02 closed with `wall-v1` calibrated (scale 2 × 200, seed 1) and `ring-v1` available but `uncalibrated`; ring readings are exposure-rung instrument readings (F04), not reachability evidence, until a competent ring comparator exists. A competent ring comparator would be a lab-authored controller or a `--comparator` genome file, which this track's comparator rule does not yet allow as the built-in; changing that is a track amendment, not a tuning of the existing controllers, the margin or the ring geometry. The scorer lesson is recorded in the F02 spec: a bounded food block saturates bites and progress, so the barrier assay grades first-reach efficiency and blocked moves, and a completed block counts as progress 1.
