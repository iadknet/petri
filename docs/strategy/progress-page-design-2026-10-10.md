# Progress Page Design (2026-10-10)

**Status**: Applied to `docs/progress/index.html` on 2026-10-10
**Scope**: the information design of the progress page only; no report,
indicator or benchmark changes.

## Problem

The owner found the page hard to read: "hard to figure out what anything
means". An audit of the page before this redesign found four causes.

- **No card explained itself.** All 34 cards had titles naming what was
  plotted ("Births and deaths per closure"). None said what the reading means,
  which direction is better, or how it bears on the goal, and every subtitle
  was a JSON path.
- **The goal was buried.** The success definition in `docs/roadmap.md` rests on
  a few readings: founder clades, lineage entropy, memory sensitivity, the
  T11/T14.F12 floor and compute cost. The page gave them the same weight as
  about 29 diagnostic charts.
- **Closures were bare IDs.** The x-axis read `T11.F23` with no name. Fifteen
  of the 44 three-world closures repeat the previous closure's readings
  exactly, because runs are deterministic and those features (measurement,
  storage, timing) did not change behaviour. They were plotted like results.
- **Encodings asked the reader to do the work.** The page used 13- and
  18-category stacked bars, and checkpoint charts with one line per closure.
  Deltas sat in captions as stored percentages against whichever closure the
  report named.

## What the page does now, and which source it rests on

1. **A verdict comes first.** One card says what the shown closure did, then
   names the first thing to check or says nothing needs attention. Three lines
   cover the goal readings and one covers the guardrails.
   - Sources: the rust-timer "overall result" line and triage summaries; Few's
     overview-then-exceptions sequence.
2. **Each reading carries a takeaway title generated by fixed rules.** Under it
   come a one-sentence plain definition, a "higher is better" or "lower is
   better" tag, and a "What is this?" panel (why it matters, how it is
   measured, caveats, stored path).
   - Sources: the Analysis Function's headline-plus-subtitle rule, the OWID
     subtitle definition, UKHSA metric documentation.
   - The sentences come from rules, not hand-written prose. This answers the
     Bach and Sarikaya caution that editorial text drifts from the data.
3. **Change chips use the arrow for direction and colour for better or worse.**
   A change smaller than the reading's typical move is grey, and every chip has
   a sentence on hover.
   - Sources: UKHSA's per-metric direction table; rustc-perf's significance
     filter; Few's guidance to highlight only exceptions.
4. **The goal readings come before everything else.** Each world shows the
   latest value, the change since the previous closure, the change since the
   series' first reading, and a sparkline over every closure.
   - The first-reading comparison is the success definition's own test.
   - Sources: the OWID table tab (start, end, change); Few's sparkline-plus-
     target pattern.
5. **Three guardrails:**
   - Worlds alive.
   - The T14.F12 floor, drawn as a bullet graph per Few's specification.
   - Compute cost from the stored `ok`/`flag`/`severe` levels, beside their
     limits, for both the long (goal) and the short (gate) run, as Bencher shows
     a result beside its limit.
6. **A closure history**, newest first, with readable names derived from
   feature slugs. Closures that changed nothing are labelled and hidden by
   default. Clicking a row, a chart point or the picker shows that closure
   everywhere.
   - Sources: rustc-perf triage ("caused by"); Grafana and Bach annotation
     patterns.
7. **Every other reading is folded by topic and drawn on first open.** Stacked
   bars became dumbbells (the shown closure against the one before, one label
   column across the three worlds), and checkpoint charts draw two runs, not 44.
   - Sources: Few's "only what is needed"; NN/g and the Analysis Function on
     avoiding stacks and keeping to about four lines per chart.
8. **Chart forms.** These follow the dataviz skill's mark and anti-pattern
   rules, added the same day.
   - History charts stack the three worlds as full-width rows on one shared
     scale, with the date axis under the last row. Side-by-side panels were
     about 240px wide on the owner's screen.
   - Goal cards are one row per world: the numbers on the left and a wide
     trend on the right.
   - History charts are stepped: each closure's reading holds until the next
     one, with lighter risers and a faint wash under the lead series, so a
     closure that changed nothing reads as flat.
   - A closure whose report is not stored is bridged in the drawing, not shown
     as a hole.
   - The x-axis carries date ticks, and a neutral shaded column marks the shown
     closure, with its value labelled.
   - Readings that boom and crash within a run (population, families) use a
     log scale, with zeros kept on the bottom edge.
   - A spread (p25–p75 brain size) is a shaded band.
   - Every chart has a Table view, and series colours follow one fixed order.
9. **A glossary of the page's words**, also shown on hover.
   - Source: NN/g's point that the page, not the reader's memory, should hold
     definitions.

## Decisions and their limits

- **Typical move.** For each reading and world it is the median size of the
  nonzero changes between consecutive closures that changed the simulation.
  It is a size reference, not a significance test. Each world is one seeded run
  and closures change behaviour on purpose, so rustc-perf's history-based noise
  model does not apply; seed replicates would be needed for one.
- **"Changed nothing".** A closure counts as having changed nothing only when
  population, births, mesh hops, clades, entropy and memory sensitivity are all
  identical to the previous closure in every world.
- **Evenness is shown as e^entropy.** It reads in families (the effective
  number, a Hill number of order 1), with the stored entropy on hover. The
  stored indicator is unchanged.
- **Older series dropped.** The `goal-v1` single-world series before T12.F04 is
  no longer drawn. Its readings remain in `docs/progress.md`, and the success
  criteria compare against the current series' first reading.
- **Deltas are computed between closures.** The page now compares each closure
  with the previous closure on the page. Cost levels still come only from the
  stored comparisons, labelled with the closure each one names.
- **Not done:** a test with a reader who did not build the page, which the
  Analysis Function, Grafana and NN/g all recommend.

## Second redesign: field record on Observable Plot (2026-10-10)

The owner found the hand-drawn SVG charts squished and ugly, even after two
passes. They asked for a different visualization system and a full redesign.
The redesign ran through the Impeccable flow:

1. A product interview, recorded in `PRODUCT.md`.
2. Two dealt direction rounds. The owner chose the **ecology field record**
   (a long-term ecological research record in the Park Grass tradition).
3. A direction contract, kept in `.impeccable/surfaces/docs-progress-index-html.md`.

### Chart library

The library was chosen on 2026-10-10 against the constraints in
`PRODUCT.md`:

- a static page with no build step;
- a vendored, pinned file under 1 MiB;
- the dark mode;
- small multiples, step and band marks, log scales, dumbbells and pointer
  interaction.

| Option | Version and size checked | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observable Plot 0.6.17 on d3 7.9.0 | 209 KB + 280 KB minified; ISC | **Chosen.** Publication-figure grammar with facets, `areaY` bands, `link` dumbbells, `symlog`, `pointer` and `scale()` for hover sync, in little code. |
| Apache ECharts 6.1.0 | about 1 MB full build; Apache-2.0 | Polished interactivity, but over the size limit unless custom-built, and dashboard-shaped. |
| Vega-Lite 6.x | about 1 MB with Vega; BSD-3 | Declarative and capable; 6.5.0 was two days old and too new for the age policy; heavier runtime. |
| Chart.js 4.5.1 | about 200 KB; MIT | No facets; dumbbells need plugins. |
| lightweight-charts (already in `frontend/`) | — | Built for financial time series; no facets, bands or categorical dumbbells. |

Remaining risk: Plot's last release was Feb 2025. The vendored copy keeps
working whatever happens upstream. Versions, licences and checksums are in
`docs/progress/vendor/README.md`, and the script tags carry SRI `integrity`
hashes.

### Visual decisions

- **Plot colours:**
  - 11 Orchards `#199e70`, 22 Canyon `#d95926`, 33 Confluence `#3987e5`.
  - These are the dataviz reference palette's first three dark steps. They
    passed the validator on the `#14181c` sheet across all pairs (worst CVD
    ΔE 9.4).
  - Green-against-orange variants failed the deuteranopia check.
- **Type:** Geist Sans and JetBrains Mono, vendored from the same packages
  the live web UI uses, so Petri's two surfaces match.
- **Structure:** the owner chose it from three built prototypes. The other
  two were a now/history split and per-plot sheets.
  1. A survey-sheet header with a ruled plot strip that brings one plot
     forward.
  2. A census matrix:
     - rows are the readings and the guardrails; columns are the plots;
     - each cell has a tabular value, a paired delta row in signed numerals
       and a census curve on a square-root scale;
     - the first census is a dashed line, and the read closure is amber;
     - notched flags on a two-tier lane name the movers.
  3. Compute cost.
  4. A closure register with runs of no-change closures folded.
  5. Supplementary charts in Plot, by topic: faceted lines, checkpoint
     columns, dumbbells and the occupancy grid.
- **Review:** an independent finish review took three passes. It found eight
  material issues, then six regressions across later batches. All are fixed,
  including squished curves, unnamed mover lines and register fit at the
  owner's 578px pane.

## Distilled around cognition and evolvability (2026-10-10)

The owner said cognition and evolvability are the two things they care about
most. The page was cut to lead with them:

- **Census matrix:** two labelled bands.
  - Cognition: memory use. It reads "Not yet" while every plot is under 1%,
    and notes that learning and prediction are not measured.
  - Evolvability: mutations that change behaviour (its first reading, at
    T14.F12, is the floor) and new behaviour after 2,000 generations of drift.
  - One "Also watched" row keeps founder families and creatures alive as values
    with a delta. Even spread and families by closure moved to the Ways of
    living topic.
- **Charts:** census curves are linear from zero. The square-root scale existed
  for the late spike in founder families, which no longer has a curve here.
  Linear keeps memory's near-zero readings flat instead of magnifying noise.
- **Mover flags:** they hang over the evolvability headline. Memory has barely
  moved, so flags scored on it would mark noise.
- **First census:** a reading added after the series began uses its own first
  measured closure.
- **Closure register:** columns are memory use, changes behaviour, floor, after
  long drift and cost.
- **Compute cost:** shows flagged counters only. The counters within limits, the
  limits and cost over time fold into one disclosure.
- **Supplementary census:** topics start with Cognition and Evolvability.
- **Data limits:**
  - Memory use is at or near 0% at almost every closure. The one exception is
    Canyon at T11.F22, at 9.3%.
  - Mutations that change behaviour exists only from T14.F12 onward.
- **Not used:** the stored helpful, neutral and detrimental mutation totals. They
  are viability-score heuristics, not a measured outcome.
