## Question

Can Petri replace imperative queue construction (push, pop, terminal execute) with a shared vote surface committed by the runtime, while making internal controller state live within the tick and mesh cycles legal, without re-creating the brittleness it is meant to remove, and how should that work be cut into features that each carry a reading?

## Verdict

**Yes, with three corrections to the proposal and one gate before it is authorized.**

1. **Adopt reset-per-pass with a per-sink rising bar** [choice, argued by construction in Section 2.3]: votes reset after every committed action and the mesh runs again from the entry, so a decision is re-made against internal state that the previous decision changed (queue, memory, integrators, weights); that is the user's rule and the only one that re-judges a plan as it grows. Its one hazard is a *static* vote, which repeats until the action cap. The bound: each commit raises that sink's own bar by one unit, so a static vote of size `v` commits `ceil(v)` times, a fresh edge commits once, a static pattern still yields its rank order as a sequence (competitive queuing), and a state-driven vote is re-judged every pass. Winner-only suppression, the earlier recommendation, is the one-shot special case and cannot re-judge.
2. **The threshold is the Terminate vote** [fact by construction, Section 2.2]: an absolute threshold is indistinguishable from a constant contribution to a Terminate sink. No `min_action_confidence` genome field is needed; heritable and runtime control of the threshold are both already expressible as votes for Terminate. The "absolute versus relative" question collapses.
3. **Budgets are not real today, so cycles are not safe to enable alone** [fact, Section 1 item 2]: the VM activity ramp resets at every node dispatch, a graph visit costs `1e-5` per compute node, and a blank detour costs nothing, so under cycles the hop cap is the only bound. "Bound runaway computation through explicit resource budgets" therefore requires a new per-tick hop ramp (the T03.F10 form lifted to the mesh) as part of the same feature that legalizes cycles.
4. **The gate**: the live survey of 2026-09-16 found the block on complexity is ecological (nothing a third node could decide is rewarded), not the action interface [fact]. The refactor may be correct and still move nothing. Section 8 specifies the T11.F15-pattern scratch probe (founder ported exactly, births probe, drift walk, Orchards seeds 11 and 12, the T11.F05 history-dependent fixtures) with predeclared directions, and Section 6 states what result would falsify the interface hypothesis. Run the probe before placing the track.

The decomposition (Section 5) is six features with one cutover: the hop ramp as a pure cost (F01); live state and legal cycles with the contract rewrite (F02); the vote surface as inert data, byte-identical trajectories (F03); the cutover, in which the runtime speaks votes and only votes, with V3Alpha1 ported exactly (F04); decision-state inputs (F05); retirement and observability (F06). T11.F10 becomes the falsification reading, run on both substrates; T18 is on hold and is redone on the new surface afterwards.

## 0. The user's requirements, as read

- **R1** [requirement] A mesh node may execute more than once in a world tick. A destination is never ineligible because it already executed. Runaway computation is bounded by explicit resource budgets only.
- **R2** [requirement] Internal controller state is live throughout the tick: memory, recurrent activity, operator state, votes, and learned weights change as their operations execute, and re-entry sees those changes. Only perception of the outside world is frozen for the tick. No obsolete tick-start internal snapshot is read or restored to enforce once-per-tick progression.
- **R3** [requirement] Creatures keep multiple actions per tick, repeated actions, and meaningful action order.
- **R4** [requirement] Paid inter-creature priority bidding stays, and stays distinct from action preferences.
- **R5** [requirement] Mesh loops, live internal state, shared memory, and within-lifetime plasticity remain expressive capabilities.
- **R6** [requirement] Computation and actions keep meaningful resource costs.
- **R7** [requirement] The evaluation reads discovery and retention of useful behavior, not controller size, execution volume, or changed-offspring counts alone.
- **R8** [requirement] The queue is built before world execution; queuing a move does not move the creature. Inputs about earlier choices distinguish queued actions from performed actions and their outcomes.

Neither of the two rejected restrictions is reintroduced anywhere in this note. Section 3 carries a self-audit line for that.

## 1. Objections and failure modes, by severity

Severity is the cost of discovering the problem after the refactor lands, not the difficulty of the fix.

### 1.1 Severe: the ecological null

[fact] The live survey (`docs/strategy/live-survey-2026-09-16.md`, Section 2.5) read 400 evolved genomes at median generation 410 and found two contributing nodes per creature, routing load-bearing in 3.3%, and 15.7% of births changing behavior. Its diagnosis was that nothing a third node could decide is rewarded in a wrap world with 54% food coverage: creatures run fixed two-to-three-action programs switched by energy and food level. T11.F21 (per-direction motor bank) and T11.F22 (meaning-stable input references) repaired the two representational blocks the survey named; both closed with no cognition claim.

[hypothesis] The vote surface removes a third representational block (imperative position and the gate-then-push construction), but it cannot create the demand for a decision. If the diagnosis holds, the refactor will read as neutral on discovery: contributing nodes stay at two, route variation stays non-load-bearing, and the delayed-cue fixtures stay undiscovered. That outcome is not a defect in the mechanism. It is the reason Section 6 pairs every reading with a control on the current substrate and states the falsifying result in advance, and the reason T02.F01 (seasons) should be read on the new substrate before the mechanism is judged.

### 1.2 Severe: budgets are not real, so cycles are not safe on their own

[fact] Today's bounds on a chain are: `max_mesh_hops` 1,024 (`crates/v3-core/src/config/simulation.rs:748`); the VM activity ramp, which resets at every node dispatch (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/vm.rs:170` sets `steps = 0` per dispatch; `docs/reference/v3-vm-isa-spec.md:135-140` states the reset and names the single-visit rule as the chain bound); a graph visit charge of `graph_node_base_cost` (`1e-5`) per compute node; a `Halt`-only VM detour at one opcode (`0.05 * 1e-6`); an unwired graph detour at zero (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/cgp/execute.rs:85`). A creature that cycles 1,000 hops through a five-node graph pays about `0.05` energy, one NoOp's worth, at a 200 cap.

[fact] The mesh note of 2026-09-06 (Section 4 item 7) measured loop-carrying lineages at 10 to 18% under drift and 0.12% under selection when loops were legal and lethal (they returned `NoOp`). T11.F15 removed loops with the visited filter rather than pricing them.

[choice] Under R1 the hop cap is the only bound. That is a *wall-clock* bound on the simulation and no *selective* bound on the creature, so a loop that commits nothing costs the population nothing and the host everything. The feature that legalizes cycles must carry a per-tick hop ramp: hop `k` in a tick pays `hop_ramp_cost * max(0, k - hop_ramp_allowance)`, the T03.F10 form lifted from VM steps to mesh hops, with the allowance set from the evolved chain-length distribution, not the founder's: the live survey read chain length median 7, p75 9, max 19 over 1,036 runs per creature (Section 2.1 of that note), so an allowance below about 20 would tax the existing goal population for reasons unrelated to cycles and move F01's cost reading; any allowance in that range is founder-neutral automatically (V3Alpha1 uses 2 hops, T18.F01's layout 6), and sustained cycling across the passes of a tick must cost a lethal share while one capped pass stays survivable (Section 2.5). Analog: the metabolic cost of sustained neural activity, which is why animals do not deliberate forever. Calibration is the spec's; the form is required, not a follow-on.

[fact] Two learning costs default to zero: `plasticity_update_cost` and `reward_learning_cost` (`crates/v3-core/src/config/simulation.rs:722-727`). Pure Hebbian updates already run per visit (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/cgp/execute.rs:226-257`). Under cycles a plastic node learns once per visit for free; the hop ramp bounds the number of visits, so it also bounds this, but the spec should say so rather than discover it.

### 1.3 Severe: a static vote under reset-per-pass repeats until the cap; the per-sink bar is the bound

[requirement] The user's rule: after each committed action the votes reset and the mesh runs again from the entry; decisions change between passes because internal state changed. [fact] Under that rule with no bound, one static edge from a frozen sensor into `Move W` commits on every pass, ten moves at `move_cost` 0.2 each (`crates/v3-core/src/config/simulation.rs:465`), against an energy decay of 0.02 per tick. Under today's single-visit rule the same edge pushes once. That is a new lethal class for "add an action" births, the class T11 spent its repairs shrinking (founder dead births 18.7% at the audit, 0.06% at the T18 probe).

[choice] The bound is a per-sink rising bar (Section 2.3): every commit raises the committed sink's bar by one unit, and a sink's effective vote is its vote minus its bar. A fresh edge (weights are drawn in [-1, 1], sensors are unit-scale since T17.F02) commits once; a static vote of size `v` commits `ceil(v)` times; a weight step of ±0.1 changes the count only at an integer crossing, which keeps the small-steps property; and a vote that reads the queue, memory, or a stateful node is re-judged on every pass against that bar; the bar is per kind, so a second direction of the same kind steers rather than adds (Section 2.3). Analog: habituation of a motor pool, the cure Maes proposed for her own network's loops (Section 7.3). The alternative, the action cap alone, is recorded in Section 4.

[fact] The mesh runs once per committed action plus one closing pass. The survey's logs show 2 or 3 actions per tick in 89% of ticks (Section 3.3 of that note), so cognition cost is expected to rise by a factor near three where plans stay that length; the hop ramp and energy price it per creature, and the wall-clock reading is predeclared in experiment 6.

### 1.4 High: fixed programs are not this surface's natural form, conditional plans are

[choice] With one commit per pass, the order of actions is the order of passes; the rank within a pass only chooses which action commits next. A static vote pattern therefore yields its rank order as a sequence of *distinct* actions with counts from magnitudes (`Eat 1.0, Move W 0.9, Move S 0.3` gives `Eat, Move W, Move S`), and every dependence of a later action on an earlier one has to come from something the plan changed: queue length, a memory slot written per pass, an integrator stepped per visit, `EnergyConsumedThisTick`. That is the cognition the queue was built to reward, and it is where a lineage pays in nodes.

[fact by construction, Section 2.3 rows W1 to W3] An exact V3Alpha1 port is expressible with static votes, inhibition, and one queue-reading node: `Eat = [food_here > 0]`, `Move[d] = 0.5 + 0.4·ring[d]` (a first-argmax over N, E, S, W because the sink order N < E < S < W matches the founder's tie order and Eat outranks every move), `Reproduce[d] = can_reproduce·(0.5 + 0.4·ring[d])` (the founder's reproduce branch also picks the food-ring maximum, `creature/founder.rs:269`), `−2·can_reproduce` into Eat and every Move, and, because live energy falls by the compute cost between passes and could cross the reproduce gate on the second pass, one `Threshold` on the queue's first slot ("a Reproduce is queued") feeding `−2` into Eat and Move and `+1` into Terminate, which closes the reproduce branch as today's terminal instruction does. The tick is `Eat, Move d`, `Move d`, or `Reproduce d`, as today; a `Constant` into the Reproduce parameter surface carries the transfer fraction. (Under a per-sink bar the three other cardinal moves would commit in the following passes; that is why the bar is per kind.) T18.F01's serial layout needs fewer nodes than on the bank because no executor node exists.

[hypothesis] The residual coupling is that a vote's weight now carries both "whether" and "how many" (integer crossings) and, within a pass, "which first" (rank crossings). The births probe reads a *reordered* class (same multiset of actions, different order) and a *recount* class (same kinds, different counts) beside changed, silent, dead, and sterile, without a predeclared direction.

### 1.5 High: the clock moves from ticks to visits

[fact] Today `execute_graph_impl` starts every visit from `tick_start_state` and `tick_start_outputs` (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/cgp/execute.rs:93-101`), captured by `GraphRuntimeState::begin_tick` in Phase 0 (`crates/v3-core/src/creature/state.rs:479-489`), and a self or higher-index edge reads the frozen tick-start output (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/cgp/sources.rs:43-52`). Eligibility traces are rebuilt from `tick_start_eligibility_traces` on every visit (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/plasticity/traces.rs`, the `base` term). This is the second rejected restriction, introduced by T11.F06 and T11.F07 to satisfy node-type contract property (3), "persistent state advances once per world tick" (`docs/reference/v3-mutation-spec.md:250-254`).

[choice] Under R2 a `DecayIntegrator(a)` steps once per *visit*, so a route mutation that adds a loop changes the effective time constant of every stateful node on the loop, and a chain that visits a node twice integrates twice. That is a new class of behavior-changing route mutations, and it is the intended capability (a loop is iteration). Analog: continuous-time recurrent controllers integrate several steps per sensorimotor step; the number of steps is part of the controller (Section 7). The consequence to state in the contract rewrite: internal time is counted in visits, world time in ticks, and unvisited modules hold. T11.F06's other guarantee, that *disconnected* growth cannot change an existing node's clock, still holds, because a disconnected node adds no visit.

[fact] The controllers in the sequencing literature that solve history-dependent tasks do so on one fixed clock with a tenfold spread between fast and slow time constants, and Beer 1995 shows switching between attractors succeeds or fails on that ratio (Section 7.2). [choice] Per-visit advance makes the ratio path-dependent where a lineage loops and leaves it unchanged where it does not. The alternative that keeps the ratio fixed is the tick-start freeze the user rejected, so it is not proposed; the consequence is recorded, and experiment 7 reads it.

[fact] The T11.F05 fixtures D1 to D3 and E1 to E3 pin per-tick advance and were written to be inverted under TDD when the semantics change (`docs/specs/roadmap/t11-f05-temporal-controller-fixtures.md`, "Fixture rule"). The memory-sensitivity indicator perturbs the `tick_start_*` substrates by name (`crates/v3-core/src/simulation/tick.rs:367-383`) and must perturb the committed `node_state` and `node_outputs` instead; its series breaks and re-bases.

### 1.6 High: the blast radius re-pins everything

[fact] 89 source and frontend files name the action bank, the execute gate, or the push/pop/execute opcodes (`grep -rl` over `crates` and `frontend/src`, 2026-09-20). `genome_size()` counts a wired action slot as one unit plus its edges (`crates/v3-core/src/creature/genome/mod.rs:287-296`); every founder's bank is unwired, so retiring the bank does not by itself move the founder anchor of 111. The founder *port* does: V3Alpha1's decision node is a 70-instruction VM program, and its vote-form replacement will not be 111 units. The per-unit mutation rate is pinned against that assertion (`crates/v3-core/src/creature/state.rs:1105-1112`), T03.F08's carrying cost and T03.F11's replication anchor read it, and T18.F02 already plans the re-anchor. Every seeded trajectory re-pins because the fixed sink catalog grows from 64 to about 90 entries and `pick_random_surface` draws uniformly over surfaces, the same draw remapping T11.F21 recorded. The T13 recruitment-path fixtures and records hand-build action banks and become historical; the `steering-v1` structural reading ("executed node writes a bank") is re-expressed as "writes a `Move` sink". None of this is an argument against the refactor; it is the price to state before the user places it.

### 1.7 Medium: repeated priority-bid payment

[fact] `SetPriorityBid` pays on every execution and keeps only the last value (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/vm.rs:457-496`: `*energy -= paid` then `side_outputs.priority_bid = paid`), with a mid-dispatch exhaustion path when the bid exceeds effective energy. Under cycles a bid inside a loop pays once per visit for one bid. [choice] Settle once: the bid is a creature-level surface (VM last-write-wins as today, a graph `PriorityBid` sink with overwrite semantics), charged once when the tick's queue closes, clamped to the energy left after compute, and Phase 2 sorts on it as now (`crates/v3-core/src/simulation/tick.rs:1218`). This keeps R4 (paid, distinct from action votes) and removes the only place the VM charges energy outside its single settlement.

### 1.8 Medium: a partial queue at energy exhaustion changes mortality

[fact] Today exhaustion during cognition discards the queue and returns `NoOp` (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/mesh.rs:355-365`), Phase 2 charges `noop_cost` 0.05, and the creature dies in Phase 2 or Phase 0. Nothing removes an exhausted creature before its actions run (`crates/v3-core/src/simulation/tick/helpers.rs:9-19` runs after each action). [choice] If committed actions survive exhaustion, a creature that committed `Eat` before running out can eat in Phase 2 and live. That is a semantic decision under R8, not a bug: committed actions were paid for and are as real as a queued move. Either answer is coherent; the note recommends keeping committed actions because the alternative makes the *order of computation* decide survival, and states that T14.F03's mortality partition will move.

### 1.9 Medium: random tie-breaking has a concrete cost

[fact] Phase 1 cognition is parallel over creatures and RNG-free (`crates/v3-core/src/simulation/tick.rs:424-517`); the only RNG is drawn sequentially in Phase 0 and Phase 2. [choice] Seeded random tie-breaking among exactly tied maxima needs per-creature RNG state in the parallel phase, its serialization, and its inheritance rule. Exact ties between evolved real-valued votes arise only from identical structure (two constants, two unwired sinks), which is the zero case. A deterministic rule costs nothing: Terminate wins its ties (when nothing beats stopping, stop), and among actions the lowest sink index wins, which is a bias only at exact ties and is the same rule the direction bank and the route resolver already use.

### 1.10 Medium: T18.F01 is designed on the surface being retired

[fact] T18.F01 (next new start) builds a founder from `RouterGate`, per-kind action slots with their gates, and a constant-gated execute gate (`docs/roadmaps/t18-founder-architecture.md`). The execute gate and the action bank do not exist after the refactor. Re-expressing its six graph nodes' gates as vote edges is mechanical, so the port itself is cheap; the real argument for folding is that its readings (router-operator sterility from `SwapRouteTargets` and `RemoveRouteTarget`, Orchards seeds 11 and 12) would be re-measured on the new surface anyway. The cost of folding is that the Orchards result the user placed T18.F01 for waits behind F01's epoch re-pin. Both orderings were coherent: T18.F01 first (Orchards sooner, one extra port later) or folded (one measurement, later). **The user's ruling of 2026-09-21: T18 is on hold until the refactor is done and is then redone on the new surface.** The refactor's founder deliverable is therefore the exact V3Alpha1 port (the control), and T18's specialized layout is re-planned afterwards on the vote surface, where it needs no executor node and its router reading changes meaning.

### 1.11 Low: the framing of "confidence" and the surface-draw dilution

[choice] Call the values votes or activations, never confidence; repeated identical contributions crossing a level is *repetition*, which under the per-sink bar means exactly "again", once per unit crossed. [fact] With about 90 sinks and a uniform surface draw, `AddGraphEdge` lands on any one named sink about 1% of the time; the mesh note's remark that "the bid's source distribution bounds the effect" applies to the destination too. A topographic bias (ring slot `d` prefers sink `d`) was recorded as a T11.F21 follow-on and stays one.

## 2. Semantics: what is coherent, what collapses, what stays open

### 2.1 The surface

[choice] One creature-level vote vector `V`, cleared at tick start (decision-local, never persistent state), one entry per sink. Sinks: `Eat[t]` per ordinary food type (one today; T17.F04's per-type bank becomes this), `Move[d]`, `Reproduce[d]`, `Steal[d]` for the eight directions, `Terminate`, and `Decide`: 27 sinks at one food type. `Terminate` and `Decide` are sinks, never committed, with no bar: Terminate ends the tick, Decide ends the pass (Section 2.4). Contributions are signed and sanitized. Each node's *latest* visit in a pass sets that node's contribution vector (a VM dispatch sums its own `AddVote`s; a graph visit's `ActionVote` sink sums its edges), and the vote is the sum of the contributions of every node visited in the pass, so two nodes add and a node revisited in a cycle re-judges rather than piles up [choice, from the adversarial review]. Inhibition is a negative contribution. `NoOp` has no sink; an empty queue at tick end is `NoOp` as today. `Pop` has no sink; a negative vote is its analog. Per-kind parameter surfaces (`Reproduce` transfer fraction, `Steal` fraction once T17.F03 lands, `Eat` type until T17.F04 makes it a bank) keep overwrite semantics, cleared at tick start, read at commit; the direction *is* the sink. This generalizes T11.F21: direction became a bid per direction there; here the action becomes a bid per (kind, direction).

### 2.2 The threshold collapses into the Terminate vote

[fact by construction] Let the commit rule be "commit the best action when its effective vote exceeds zero and, once something is queued, exceeds `V[Terminate]`". An absolute threshold `θ` is then a constant contribution `θ` to `Terminate`, which a graph expresses as `Constant(θ) → Terminate` and a VM as `LoadConst; AddVote{Terminate}`; a runtime-manipulated threshold is any other edge into that sink. A lead-over-alternatives rule is already the argmax. So there is no separate threshold concept, no `min_action_confidence` field, and "absolute versus relative" is not a decision. The zero baseline matches today's gates (`wsum > 0` fires) and the bank (a bid must be positive to beat the scalar decode). The user's worry that threshold and vote magnitude are interchangeable is correct and is why only one of them exists. Two limits of the Terminate vote: it cannot end a tick before anything is queued (Section 2.8's guard), and a negative Terminate vote only cancels positive Terminate contributions; it never makes a non-positive action vote commit, because the `e <= 0` stop is unconditional.

### 2.3 The commit rule and what resets

[choice, recommended; the user's rule with the bound the user chose on 2026-09-21, made per kind after the first adversarial review, written as a transition contract after the second] A tick is a sequence of passes. Each pass runs the mesh from the entry with the votes cleared; at the end of the pass the action kind with the largest effective vote commits one action if that vote is positive and, once something is queued, beats the Terminate vote; that kind's bar rises by one unit; the next pass begins. Internal state is never reset. The four action kinds are Eat, Move, Reproduce, and Steal; the sinks inside a kind (a direction, a food type) compete by argmax, which is T11.F21's rule kept. Terminate and Decide are sinks, not kinds: neither is committed and neither has a bar. Read the kind vote as "how many", the within-kind argmax as "which", Decide as "I am ready", and Terminate as "I am done".

```text
tick start: V[s] = 0 for every sink, C[K] = 0 for the four action kinds, queue empty, bus zeroed
pass:
  run the mesh from the entry (bus carried from the last node of the previous pass; internal state live)
    V[s] = sum over nodes visited this pass of that node's latest contribution to s, updated at every node boundary
    after each node's effects: if V[Decide] > 0 and the pass end below would not be NoDecision, end the pass here (Decided)
    the pass also ends at NoTargets, MissingNode, or the per-pass hop cap (PassCapReached), all of which keep V and the queue
  at pass end:
    for K in {Eat, Move, Reproduce, Steal}:
        best[K] = argmax over K's sinks of V[s]  (ties: lowest sink index)
        e[K]    = V[best[K]] - C[K] * unit        (unit = 1.0)
    (K*, e) = argmax e[K]                         (ties: the kind committed in the previous pass, then lowest kind index)
    t = V[Terminate]
    if e <= 0:                      end the tick (NoDecision)
    if queue non-empty and t >= e:  end the tick (TerminateVoted)
    commit best[K*] with the current parameter surface of K*
    C[K*] += 1
    if queue full:                  end the tick (ActionCapReached)
    V[s] = 0 for every sink; next pass
```

What the rows establish:

- **One edge adds one action or steers, with one stated exception.** Into an unused kind, exactly one action (W4). Into a used kind's other sink, a steer (W5). Into a sink the genome already votes on, it raises that vote and therefore the count and the rank (W6); that is magnitude-as-count doing its job, and the track's acceptance criterion says so rather than claiming a universal guarantee.
- **The kind vote is how many; the argmax is which.** `ceil(v)` actions of a kind (W7), every one in the direction that wins its pass; a second action of any kind needs a vote above one unit (W6, W9), so a route's length is the size of its Move vote and its turns are state-driven direction changes (W9).
- **Planning is a construction, as it should be.** W8 and W9 are two or three nodes reading the queue; the pass is the loop and no mesh cycle is needed. Under the one-shot rules these plans do not exist; under reset-all without the bar, W4 and W7 run to the action cap.
- **A cycle re-judges, it does not pile up, and it has two exits of its own.** W11 to W14 and W17: contribution replacement keeps a static cycle from inflating a vote; a counter on the bus can still inflate it by design; a route exit needs a second target, because the resolver never returns "no target" while a target exists; a Decide exit is one edge from any state the loop changes. W18: the Decide guard makes a stray Decide edge harmless.
- **Deliberation is tunable without a register.** W19: a constant sets the heritable readiness a creature needs before it acts, a memory slot or an integrator moves it with state and persists across ticks, and Section 2.2's rule holds for Decide as it does for Terminate: a threshold is a contribution. Analog: tonic neuromodulation of the response threshold, lowered by hunger (Section 7.4's urgency signal, Section 7.3's dopamine level).
- **Terminate is bounded on both sides.** It cannot end an empty tick (W15) and a negative Terminate vote cannot commit a non-positive action (the `e <= 0` stop).
- **A tick-scoped working memory for free.** The bus (`upstream_slots`) is zeroed at tick start and carried from the last node of one pass to the entry of the next [choice, user decision 2026-09-21]. `slot0 += 1` is a pass counter (W9's "moves queued" can be read from it as well as from the queue) and `slot1 += direction` is dead reckoning, and both clear themselves when perception refreshes; a per-tick counter in shared memory would need a clear every tick, two coordinated mutations for one scratch value. An unwritten slot reads the previous pass's value instead of zero, which is itself a "not the first pass" signal. No second bus: any node that wants fresh data has a predecessor that writes it every pass.

What resets and what does not: `V` at every pass and at tick start; `C` (per kind), the parameter surfaces, and the bus at tick start; nothing internal ever. The split is then clean: bus within the tick, shared memory across ticks, graph state and learned weights always. The `unit` is a fixed body constant equal to the vote scale (1.0), not a genome field; the creature's Terminate vote is the heritable and runtime modifier, positive to stop sooner, and it can be lowered by a negative contribution only back toward zero. Connection weights, learned weights, memory, operator state, traces, and the queue are untouched by a pass boundary.

### 2.4 When votes are evaluated

[choice, revised 2026-09-21 at the user's request] At the end of a pass only, and a pass ends in one of four ways: the chain ends (`NoTargets`, `MissingNode`), the per-pass hop cap of Section 2.5 fires (`PassCapReached`), or the genome ends it (`Decided`): after any node's effects, if the summed `Decide` vote is positive and the pass-end decision would do something (some kind's effective vote is positive, or the queue is non-empty and Terminate is positive), the pass ends at that node's boundary and the ordinary pass-end decision runs on what has been voted so far. All four keep the votes and the queue. The runtime never decides early on its own, which keeps the early-commit objection answered: an early `Decide` is the genome's choice, exactly as an early terminal instruction is today, and the guard makes a stray `Decide` edge harmless (W18) the way the Terminate guard does. `Decide` is the pass-level counterpart of Terminate: "I am ready" against "I am done". Two alternatives were rejected: an exact-equality stall detector (end the pass when a revisited node sees bit-identical votes and bus) is unsound, because the votes and the bus are not the live state, a `DecayIntegrator` steps on every visit with identical votes and energy falls by the ramp on every hop and is a live input, so the check either excludes those and is wrong or includes them and never fires, and it is the runtime ending a pass on its own; and an epsilon convergence rule is T11.F06's hidden clock. A route exit through `AddRouteTarget` is not the cheap alternative it looks: the operator's detour forwards to the same successor, inside the loop, so an exit needs the detour retargeted to a chain end plus a gate on state, two or three coordinated events against one edge. Restart from the entry after each commit is the proposal's step 3 taken literally. Mesh cycles inside a pass are computation (iteration, memory, a self-loop that settles), bounded by the hop ramp; they are not how sequences are made. Terminate wins only against a non-empty queue (Section 2.8) and ends the tick; a pass with no positive effective vote ends the tick as `NoDecision`, which is `NoOp` when nothing was committed, the same outcome an idle founder produces today.

### 2.5 Budgets and partial queues

[choice, sharpened by the adversarial review] With the visited filter gone, a node with targets always routes, so a static cycle (`A → B → A`) inside a pass ends only at a cap. That cap must not be death, or every looping lineage (10 to 18% under drift in the 2026-09-06 note) dies at F02 and the track retreats toward a visit limit. So: **the per-pass hop cap** (`max_mesh_hops`, re-scoped to one pass and lowered by the spec, above the survey's maximum chain of 19) **ends the pass, keeps the votes and the queue, and hands the decision to the pass end**; one capped pass is survivable and paid through the ramp. **The per-tick hop ramp** (Section 1.2) accumulates over every pass, so a creature that cycles to the pass cap on every pass of every tick pays a lethal share over a few ticks, the T03.F10 standard. The evolvable exits from a cycle are a `Decide` vote from state the loop changes (one edge, Section 2.4) or a route gate to a second target reading the same state (two or three events); the cap is the safety net for lineages that have neither, priced by the ramp, and the births probe reads the cost rather than assuming it. Four bounds then end a tick: the action cap `max_actions_per_turn` (10), which also bounds passes at the cap plus one; the ramp's energy; the pass cap ending every pass of a tick that never decides (`NoDecision` once no positive kind vote remains); and energy exhaustion, the Section 1.8 decision. `mutation.action_queue_cap` (4) retires with the bank; it also sizes the `ActionQueue` compound input's width (`crates/v3-core/src/mutation/compound.rs:11`, `cap * 3`), which F06 fixes as a constant or predeclares as a remap.

### 2.6 Live state

[choice] A graph visit starts from the committed `node_state` and `node_outputs` (the last successful visit, this tick or an earlier one) and commits on success; a self or higher-index edge reads the last committed output (synchronous update per visit). What a visit that runs out of energy leaves behind is per substrate, and today's code already differs across them (`crates/v3-core/src/runtime/cgp/execute.rs:226-274` applies pure Hebbian weight changes before the plasticity-cost exhaustion check and never rolls them back; the mesh spec says so): learned weights, applied and kept; operator state and outputs, not committed; graph effects (memory writes, votes, parameters), not applied; a VM dispatch's memory copy, not committed; the bus, unchanged; committed actions and the settled priority bid, Section 1.8's choice (keep). F02 owns the state rows and F04 the queue and bid rows, and each writes them into the mesh spec's exhaustion section. Eligibility: `trace += activity` per successful visit, decayed once per world tick in Phase 0, which is elapsed world time and not an internal freeze. Pure Hebbian updates are already per visit. Shared memory is live and committed per dispatch as today. `prev_shared_memory` (`LoadSlotPrev`, `SharedMemory { previous: true }`) is a delay line, "what my memory held when the tick began"; it does not restore or freeze current memory. **Kept, by the user's ruling of 2026-09-21**: it is an input by role even though it is a tick-start copy by mechanism, and current memory stays live. VM registers, `meta`, and the direction bank stay dispatch-local; they are scratch, not the persistent state R2 names.

### 2.7 Inputs

[choice, user decisions 2026-09-21] Keep the `ActionQueue` compound input and the VM queue reads; they are R8's "previously queued". Add, all cleared at tick start and none touching the world:

- `ActionVotes`: the current pass's vote vector, one sub-value per sink, resolved live like `ActionQueue`, so a node can inhibit relative to the current leader (lateral inhibition circuits).

Considered and declined by the user: a derived queued-displacement input (net dx, dy of the queued moves). Dead reckoning stays a construction on the persistent bus. Immediate world feedback between passes stays a separate proposal, as the user said.

**A. Reset per pass with a per-sink rising bar, decisions at pass end, Terminate sink, per-tick hop ramp** (adopted, Section 2.3). **B. Reset per pass, action cap only** (the proposal as written). Same planning semantics; a static edge repeats to the cap, so "add an action" births become expensive at birth (Section 1.3). Kept as the fallback if the probe reads the bar as harmful.

**C. One-shot ranking: winner-only suppression or unit debit, no re-run.** One traversal yields the rank order as a sequence (competitive queuing) and, with unit debit, counts from magnitudes; cheapest; but the plan is never re-judged, so conditional navigation needs an explicit mesh cycle that re-casts votes, plus a self-built position estimate. Good for fixed programs, which the live survey found is all the population does today; wrong center for the north-star goal. Row four and five of the Section 2.3 table.

**D. Factored votes: kind sinks plus one shared direction bank.** Five kind sinks and eight direction bids. Fewer surfaces (13 versus 26), but a direction bid affects every movement kind at once and two directions of one kind cannot be ranked in one pass. Rejected for R3; recorded because it is the smallest surface.

**E. Graded all-actions-every-tick with a fixed physiological order, no queue** (the Polyworld form, Section 7.2). Every kind executes every tick with an intensity; order is fixed by the body. The most evolvable known form for single-action-per-kind behavior, and it removes R3 and the planning goal. Rejected; it is the fallback if the probe shows the queue itself is the problem.

**F. Minimal change: keep imperative pushes, retire single-visit and the freeze, add the ramp, and let T18.F01 seed per-kind gated nodes.** No vote surface; a gate fires and pushes as today. Cheapest; keeps the binary gate discontinuity and the position-addressed bank. This is Section 5's F01 alone. It is the control arm for the probe and the stopping point if the probe falsifies the interface hypothesis.

**G. Persistent routing position (a sticky node that resumes next tick)**. The mesh note's C3; orthogonal to selection, deferred there until T11.F10 reports. Unchanged.

The recommendation is A, built as F first (F01) and then the surface (F02), so that the substrate with cycles and live state is read once on its own before the interface changes.

Readings are the existing instruments; nothing new is invented.

1. **One-edge reachability census** (new, cheap). For the founder and for 36 goal-sampled genomes, apply every single edge from every sensor sub-value to every action surface (old: slot gates and bank bids; new: vote sinks), one at a time, and classify the birth on the neighborhood battery: silent, changed, reordered, recount, dead, sterile. Five sub-censuses on the new surface, each a Section 2.3 row: a unit-weight edge into an unused kind (W4: exactly one added action), the same edge into a used kind's other sink (W5: a steer), the same edge into a sink already voted on (W6: a recount and a reorder, counted as such), the unused-kind edge at weight 3 (W7: three), and a `Threshold` on queue length into Terminate added to a repeating genome (W10: the plan stops). 2. **Births probe** (T11.F14 founder half; the founder-refactor probe's method). 4,000 births per founder. 3. **Drift walk** to generations 1,000 and 2,000 (`drift-depth-v4` after F01). Predeclared: changed per all births at 2,000 not below the standing 0.005; hop-cap hits reported against a stated ceiling with their ramp cost; executed, contributing, and knockout counts.

## 7. What established systems do

Four reading agents read the primary texts in full where a full copy could be obtained; the Sources list states, per text, whether it was read in full. Every line here is [fact] from the text unless marked [inference].

### 7.1 Graph-program and digital-organism systems (loops, budgets, multiple outputs)

- **Tangled program graphs** (Kelly and Heywood 2017, EuroGP Section 4.2 and GECCO Algorithm 2; Kelly, Smith, Heywood and Banzhaf 2021, TELO Section 2.2 and Figure 3): cycles may exist in the graph but are never followed; a team is visited at most once per decision, a winning bid that points to a visited team falls through to the runner-up, and every team keeps at least one atomic-action program, enforced at *variation* time, so a decision always reaches one action. The interface is one atomic action per decision by argmax bid. No paper in the lineage ablates single-visit against a bounded revisit. [inference] T11.F15 imported the visit rule but not its companion, the variation-time guarantee that a chain always ends in an action; under R1 the companion is what a Terminate sink and the action cap supply.
- **MATPG** (Vacher et al. 2026, Section 3): the same argmax traversal with no visited set, cycle handling, or budget stated; action vertices emit one continuous value per action class, at most one program per class, missing classes filled by a default, and the six torques leave as one simultaneous unordered vector. Its gain over flat agents (Table 1, Cohen d 2.2 to 2.7) needed lexicase selection with task labels and a genuinely multi-context task; under tournament selection flat agents matched it as obstacles grew (Section 6). [inference] Hierarchy and routing paid only where the task demanded several contexts, which is the ecological-null point of Section 1.1 in another system's numbers.
- **Genetic Network Programming** (Mabu, Hirasawa and Hu 2007, abstract only; mechanics from four open same-lab texts: Katagiri et al. 2002 Section 2.1, Mabu et al. 2006 Section 2.1, Zhou et al. 2007 Equations 1 and 3, Chen et al. 2009 Section 3.2): judgment and processing nodes are traversed with no terminal node, so the current node carries across time steps (the implicit memory); each node and edge has a delay, a step ends when accumulated delay reaches a threshold (5 in 2002, 10 in 2006) and the counter resets; processing nodes act in traversal order, several per step under the budget; a step can spend its whole budget on judgments and emit nothing; a fitness term penalizes loop count after the fact. Two vote-like commits exist: a processing node averaging the outputs of every judgment node traversed since the last processing node and committing against a threshold, and an argmax over weighted scores accumulated along the traversal. [inference] GNP is the closest precedent to a budgeted, revisitable mesh with ordered multiple actions per step, and its "empty step" is the case Section 2.4's pass-end decision and Section 2.3's zero baseline handle explicitly.
- **SignalGP regulation** (Lalejini, Moreno and Ofria 2021, Sections 4.1 to 4.5, 5.2 and 5.3): concurrent threads run round-robin, each signal gets 128 steps, then all threads reset and thread-local memory is lost; a response instruction halts every thread, one response per signal. Regulation helped on the context-dependent problems (signal counting 137 versus 8 of 200 solved at two signals; contextual 173 versus 200; prefix calculator 30 versus 66 in the opposite direction on one problem) and hurt where the task did not need it: on the independent-signal task both arms solved 200 of 200 but 18 of 200 regulated solutions failed to generalize versus 0, five of them generalizing once regulation was knocked out, and the postfix calculator solved 120 versus 151. The harm appeared under single-ordering evaluation. [inference] Petri's one-life world is single-ordering evaluation, so gates, votes, and cycles that selection does not test will accumulate cryptic variation; experiments 3 and 5 read that directly.
- **Avida** (Ofria and Wilke 2004, Sections 2.1, 2.2.1, 3.1, 4.2, 5.2): a circular sequential program with loops through head movement and no visit exclusion; an update is when the average organism has executed 30 instructions, allocated by merit; per-instruction costs are configurable; halting is undecidable so the test environment uses a timeout; outputs are sequential writes scored by the environment, one reward per computation. [inference] Avida is the existence proof that a budget (cycles priced by merit) rather than exclusion bounds an evolving program, and that no arbitration among competing actuators is needed when outputs are sequential writes.

Across these five: no system read combines visit exclusion with a compute budget; the TPG lineage uses exclusion plus a structural action guarantee, GNP and Avida use budgets and allow revisits freely; only MATPG and GNP emit several actuator outputs per step, one unordered and one in traversal order; and no system read lets arbitrary nodes add votes to shared sinks, so that part of the proposal has precedent in the decision literature (Section 7.2) rather than in evolutionary systems.

### 7.2 Evolved recurrent controllers and history-dependent choice (clocks and outputs)

- **Yamauchi and Beer 1994** (Adaptive Behavior 2(3), Sections 2.1, 3, 4; read in full from Beer's Indiana page): one shared forward-Euler clock (step 1, time constants in [1, 70]); an environment event is a 10-update pulse at 10 to 40-update intervals, so the controller takes tens of updates per event and its state is never frozen or reset within a trial. Output is one graded neuron scored as an integral over the trigger window (Equation 3); no threshold, no argmax. Sequence generation ((10)* and (110)* under identical triggers) was solved by basin switching between trigger-off attractors and by a limit cycle plus a slow region (Figures 2 and 3); the sequence position is which basin or phase the state occupies, not a counter. Two-bit learning could not be evolved directly and was seeded by composing two five-neuron solutions (Section 4.3). No success rates are reported.
- **Izquierdo, Harvey and Beer 2008** (Adaptive Behavior 16(6), Sections 2 to 4; read in full): Euler step 0.1, trial phases of 100 to 240 updates on the same clock, state reset only at trial start; one graded effector scored by a windowed integral (Equation 3). The test stimulus alone does not determine the answer; history does. Discrete task: 4 of 20 three-node and 12 of 20 four-node runs passed 99% after 10,000 generations; continuum task: 0 of 20 at three and four nodes, 12 of 20 and 13 of 20 at five and six nodes after 20,000. The strobed states form a finite-state machine but are transients, "always being pulled from one attractor to the next"; evolution split the time constants into about 1 and about 10 times slower, and the slow nodes carry the trace. Memory decays without re-exposure.
- **Beer 1995** (Adaptive Behavior 3(4); read in full): a self-excited neuron is bistable iff its self-weight exceeds 4 (Section 2.1); only the ratio of time constants matters for the phase portrait, and one change of ratio moves a three-neuron circuit between a limit cycle, a torus, and chaos (Figure 9); a bistable neuron driven too fast falls back before reaching its upper attractor (Figure 10). Evolvability is argued, not measured: a random network is overwhelmingly monotone, and richer dynamics need coordinated weights.
- **Polyworld** (Yaeger 1994, Sections 6, 8, 12; read in full): exactly one synchronous activation pass and one Hebbian pass per world step, chosen for biological parallelism; "multiple internal, neural time-cycles per external, action time-cycle" is listed as unrealized future work. Seven behaviors (eat, mate, fight, move, turn, focus, light) have one prespecified output neuron each, all read every step: eat, mate, and fight need a threshold and then scale with activation, the rest are linear in activation; there is no selection among behaviors and the within-step order is not stated.

What these establish for Petri: [fact] none of the four runs several controller updates per environment step on a decoupled clock; the two that show history-dependent choice under identical current input do it with state that persists across events and a spread of at least ten between fast and slow time constants, on one fixed clock. [inference] Persistence is the necessary part of R2; several visits per tick is an addition these papers neither test nor need. [inference] Under per-visit advance, `N` visits in a tick divide every effective time constant on the visited nodes by `N`, so the slow-versus-fast separation these results depended on becomes path-dependent where a lineage loops; for a DAG-shaped lineage (every founder, and every genome in the survey's contributing core) nothing changes. That is recorded in Section 1.5 as the price of R2, and experiment 7 reads whether looped stateful nodes act as memory or as delay. [fact] Every output in these papers is graded and read as a window integral or a per-step magnitude; none uses argmax or votes. [inference] A pass-end decision reads the votes accumulated over the whole chain rather than an instant, which is the window-integral reading the transient-state result argues for.

### 7.3 Animat action selection (thresholds, resets, persistence, dithering)

- **Maes 1989** (AI Memo 1180, the memo text of the Connection Science paper; Sections 2, 5, 6; the 1990 paper's algorithm section read in a Japanese translation, the English original being paywalled): modules accumulate activation from state, goals, and predecessor and successor links; a module fires when it is executable, above a global threshold, and maximal; ties are random; **the winner is reset to zero and losers keep their activation** (footnote 3: otherwise a module "could become active a couple of times in a row"); if nothing fires the threshold drops 10% and is restored on the next selection. One module per time step; sequences arise because the world changes and re-feeds the network. Failure modes: loops and deadlocks in about 8% of parameter settings that no tuning removes, a low threshold that jumps between goals, parameters that are problem- and size-dependent, and a threshold that must rise with network size (Section 6.3). Maes's own cure for loops is habituation (a per-module threshold that rises after each firing), not a reset.
- **Tyrrell 1993** (thesis, Chapters 8 to 12; read): an extended free-flow hierarchy with winner-take-all only at the final common path, one action per time step, nothing persisting after a selection. His critique of Maes: dividing activation by the number of recipients penalizes shared nodes and no division rule can work (pp. 186 to 191); binary sensors lose information; and **no persistence, since the winner restarts at zero while losers retain activation** (p. 196). Dithering appears whenever drives lack external stimuli; a GA over his parameters "proved impossible" (p. 162); robustness is asserted, not lesion-tested.
- **Gurney, Prescott and Redgrave 2001 I and II; Prescott et al. 2006** (read): scalar salience per channel, leaky integrators with a positive threshold, diffuse excitation summed over channels, selection when a channel's tonic inhibition falls below a level; no reset, continuous dynamics; persistence comes from biasing the contest toward the current winner (hysteresis plus busy signals), adopted over a mutex. Selection fails at low salience, distorts when two high saliences match (dithering; an oscillation trap under a uniform salience boost), and degrades as active channels increase; capacity scales only through a feedback loop. Soft co-selection of non-conflicting channels is simultaneous, not ordered. Lesioning one loop abolished selection until a weight was hand-retuned (GPR II Figure 2).
- **Blumberg 1994** (Hamsterdam; read): mutual inhibition with gains above 1 converges to winner-take-all with hysteresis; fatigue rises while active and decays when idle, forcing time-sharing; low gains dither and high gains persist at the cost of opportunism; disabling losers' recommendations to the winner caused a move/avoid oscillation and early death (1,600 versus 6,400 steps). A genome exists but was never evolved.

What these establish for Petri: [fact] every mechanism read emits one selection per evaluation, and ordered sequences arise only after the world changes; the one within-evaluation multi-action rule (GPR's soft selection) is simultaneous and restricted to non-conflicting resources. [fact] Maes's zero-the-winner-keep-the-losers rule is the one-shot form Section 2.3 rejects in favor of her own proposed cure, per-module habituation, which the per-sink rising bar is; Tyrrell's objection to the zeroing rule is about persistence *across* time steps. [inference] Petri separates the two: votes are decision-local and cleared at tick start, so across-tick persistence is not the vote surface's job; it belongs to stateful nodes and memory, where one mutation (a `DecayIntegrator` inserted on a sensor-to-sink path) buys hysteresis under genome control. A carried-over vote vector with a global leak and a winner bonus (the Blumberg and Prescott form) is the alternative; it is recorded in Section 2.8 and not recommended, because its time constant would be a hidden, non-evolvable parameter. [fact] Dividing activation by contributor count is where Tyrrell found unfixable brittleness; Petri's votes are additive and never normalized. [fact] A global absolute threshold drifts in meaning as the network grows (Maes Section 6.3, GPR Result 2); Petri has none, its baseline is zero, and the bound on promiscuous commits is the action cap and the world's action costs. [inference] The dithering and promiscuity readings these authors define (decisiveness, margin between the top two, switch rate) are cheap per-pass indicators and are added to experiment 6. [fact] No paper read measures behavior under link mutation; the literature supports no evolvability claim for any selection rule, which is why Section 6 measures it directly.

### 7.4 Decision and serial-order models (accumulators, thresholds, competitive queuing)

- **Usher and McClelland 2001** (leaky competing accumulator; author preprint, read in full): each alternative accumulates its input minus a leak minus lateral inhibition from the others, floored at zero (Equations 2 and 4); a free-response choice is the first unit to reach an absolute criterion, and lateral inhibition is what makes a per-unit absolute criterion behave as a relative one and extend to many alternatives (pp. 4 to 5). Feed-forward inhibition is rejected because with more than two partially supported units every net input can go negative (p. 5). Accumulators restart at zero between trials; nothing resets within one. Leak-dominant dynamics weight recent evidence, inhibition-dominant weight early evidence; a fixed criterion across set sizes raises errors with the number of alternatives and can stall (p. 53). Ties are settled by noise.
- **Bullock and Rhodes 2003; Bullock 2004** (competitive queuing; author preprint and the BU technical report, read in full): a planning layer holds graded activations (a primacy gradient), a choice layer runs winner-take-all, the winner launches its action and an inhibitory feedback path zeroes that plan, and the competition re-runs until the field is empty; deleting an item preserves the rank order of the rest. Repeats are not handled in the basic model; capacity for reliably ordered items is small under normalization plus noise; the signature errors are adjacent transpositions and tail omissions; the evidence is pre-movement cortical activity whose rank predicts stroke order. Ward's generator variant settles, selects the maximum, inhibits the inputs it expressed, and iterates. A recurrent choice field needs a high downstream threshold to prevent premature multiple activations; feed-forward competition avoids that.
- **Bogacz, Brown, Moehlis, Holmes and Cohen 2006** (read in full): the drift-diffusion model ends at symmetric absolute thresholds on the *difference* (Equation 5); the linearized accumulator's difference is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process whose sign of leak-minus-inhibition decides whether errors floor (inhibition-dominant, early noise decides) or decision times diverge (leak-dominant); a per-unit absolute threshold maps to a difference threshold that falls as total input rises (Equation 26); the pure race is slowest at fixed error (Figure 10b). Threshold and gain are not interchangeable (the reward-rate-optimal threshold is non-monotone in drift, Figure 12), though joint scaling of drift, threshold, and noise leaves outcomes unchanged. Two alternatives only; no serial order; no mechanism for forcing a decision at an interrogation time (p. 716).
- **Cisek, Puskas and El-Murr 2009** (urgency gating; PMC full text, read in full): evidence is low-pass filtered and multiplied by a choice-independent urgency signal that grows with time, against a constant commitment level (Equation 2), so the evidence needed to commit falls with time; a rising gain against a fixed threshold is a collapsing bound. Human data on changing evidence show no memory of early evidence and reject an explicit integrator reset (Figures 5 and 7). One task; the urgency function is unspecified.

What these establish for Petri: [fact] every one of these models has a noise term, and its speed-accuracy, stalling, and set-size results are about integrating noise; Petri's cognition is deterministic and its perception is frozen for the tick, so within a tick a "race" is just the argmax and those results do not transfer. [fact] The models agree on the *across-trial* rule: accumulators restart when the evidence changes; and the *within-trial* rule: nothing resets. [inference] That is the split Section 2.3 makes: votes clear at every pass, because between passes the evidence the votes read (the plan and the internal state) has changed, and accumulate within a pass, where nothing has. [fact] Serial order from one graded pattern (competitive queuing) requires a loaded gradient, deletion of the winner, and termination on an empty field, with repeats needing a re-activation. [inference] The per-sink rising bar is competitive queuing with the winner "deleted" by its own bar instead of by zeroing, re-run each pass: a static pattern yields its rank order, a static vote above one unit means "again" (R3's repeated action, bounded by the bar and the cap), and a vote that reads the plan is re-judged, which the one-shot form cannot do. [fact] Feed-forward inhibition (signed negative votes) can drive every alternative below zero. [inference] Petri's rule then commits nothing in that pass and the tick ends with whatever was committed, or `NoOp`; that is a coherent outcome, not a stall, because there is no noise to wait for. [fact] No model establishes how a decision is forced at a deadline; urgency gating makes the deadline a rising gain the decider can feel. [inference] The per-sink bar is urgency in reverse for repeats (each further repeat needs a stronger drive), the hop ramp is its economic form for deliberation, and exposing hops-so-far this tick as a dynamic introspection input (beside `EnergyConsumedThisTick`, which already rises per pass) would let a lineage evolve its own collapsing bound by voting Terminate on it; recorded as an F03 candidate. [fact] All four settle ties by noise. [inference] Petri's deterministic tie rule (Section 2.8) is the replacement, and an exact tie resolves in the pass where it occurs.

## 8. The cheap proof (scratch-worktree probe specification)

The T11.F15 pattern: a counterfactual in a scratch worktree, nothing committed, same seeds, read with the existing probes. It is the one next action this note recommends before the track is placed.

- **Patch**: F01's executor change (filter out, committed-state reads, per-visit traces, hop ramp at a provisional `allowance 16, cost 1e-4`), and a graph-only vote surface (sinks, pass-based decisions with the per-sink rising bar, Terminate, the bus carried between passes) with push/pop left in place for VM nodes, so the founder can be ported as a graph-only two-node genome (sense node unchanged; decision node re-expressed as vote edges) and V3Alpha1's VM control stays runnable.
- **Verification of the port**: the founder tests' nine scenarios and the 2,000-ring direction check must be identical to V3Alpha1.
- **Readings**: experiments 1, 2, 3 (to 1,000), 5 (B1, B2, alternation), 7, and Orchards seeds 11 and 12 to 2,000 ticks, each beside the pre-patch control from the same seed.
- **Predeclared**: dead and sterile per birth not up; single-edge changed-and-viable up and a unit-weight edge commits once; alternation discovered in more lineages on the patch than on the control; passes per tick and cognition wall per creature-tick reported against a stated ceiling; Orchards seed 11 direction read, not predeclared (one seed).
- **Budget**: the births probe and fixtures run in seconds to minutes; two Orchards seeds are about 15 minutes each; the patch is on the order of a day of implementation.

If the probe falsifies experiment 5 and experiment 1 together, Section 4's option E (F01 alone) is the stopping point and the interface is not the block.

## 9. Remaining uncertainty

- The ecological null (Section 1.1) is the largest: the mechanism can be right and the reading flat. Seasons (T02.F01) are the demand-side lever and are sequenced after the reading, not before.
- Winner-only suppression is argued by construction and by precedent, not measured here; the probe measures it.
- The hop ramp's constants are unmeasured; the founder-neutral allowance is the only hard constraint.
- The reordered-birth class is new and its ecological weight (does order matter in these worlds?) is unread; experiment 2 records it without a direction.
- The memory literature's results rest on a fixed internal-to-external clock ratio and on reaching a bistable regime (self-weight above 4 in Beer's units). Whether Petri's operator families reach the equivalent regime for `DecayIntegrator`, `Momentum`, and self-edged `Sigmoid` nodes, and whether loops help or hurt that, is unmeasured; T11.F10 is where it is read.
